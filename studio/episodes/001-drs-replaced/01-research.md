# Research — EP 01
Season context: 2026 FIA Formula 1 regulations as applied at 2026-10-16. That includes the WMSC changes approved early in the season (low-grip partial aero, Overtake disabled in low grip) and the April 2026 package that took effect from the Miami GP on 1–3 May (Boost capped at +150 kW, MGU-K held at 350 kW only in key acceleration zones, recharge can be cut to 7 MJ per event). DRS figures are for 2011–2025.

Method note: this session's WebFetch could not reach any host (DNS failure, even on example.com), and the proxy blocked direct downloads of fia.com, racefans.net, the-race.com and motorsport.com. - F3: "2026: every car, every straight. Both wings open." Do not say the change is automatic. The driver presses a button.
- F4: "Straight Mode saves energy. It isn't a passing aid."
- F5: "The FIA picks the zones track by track. Monaco: zero."
- F6: Not needed for this episode. Keep it for the "Partial aero in the wet" episode.
- F7: "Within 1 s at the detection line, you get it for the next lap." Avoid "anywhere on the lap" (see Unverified).
- F8: "+0.5 MJ: the chaser can harvest more energy every lap." For a counter, use "0.5 MJ". Don't call it a free battery top-up, because it raises the recharge limit.
- F9: "Normally, electric power starts fading above 290 km/h."
- F10: "In Overtake Mode: full 350 kW up to about 337 km/h." The chart can use two sourced points per line: normal, full to 290 km/h then falling; Overtake, full to about 337 km/h and zero at 355 km/h. Label the chart `ILLUSTRATIVE` unless it plots the formulas exactly. Do not label a zero point on the normal line (see Unverified).
- Payoff: "Between 290 and about 337 km/h, only the chaser keeps full electric power." This is derived from F9 and F10, so state it as a comparison of the two curves. It does not claim a speed gap in km/h.
- F11: If the 350 kW figure appears, add nothing that implies 350 kW everywhere on the lap. The safe phrase is "up to 350 kW".
- F14: "Monza 2026, Verstappen: passed 'like a turtle'." Credit the quote to Verstappen, after the race.

## Misconception to correct
"Straight Mode is the new DRS." It isn't. DRS gave a drag advantage only to the car within 1 s. Straight Mode gives the same drag cut to everyone in every zone, so it creates no relative advantage (F3, F4). The 1-second rule survives, but it now controls Overtake Mode, an electrical advantage (F7, F8).

Second misconception, useful for accuracy: "Overtake Mode is a battery top-up." The FIA event documents set it as a higher recharge limit: 9.0 vs 8.5 MJ at Miami, 7.5 vs 7.0 MJ at Monza. It also comes with a deployment curve that holds full power to a higher speed (F8, F10). Phrase it as "harvest more, deploy longer", not "free energy".

## Real example
2026 Italian GP, Monza, 6 Sep 2026 (F14). Verstappen led briefly. Afterwards he said cars behind him in Overtake Mode passed him "like a turtle" and that he could not defend, even on better tyres. This is a driver's own account of the episode's mechanism, sourced to three outlets. It does not name a specific lap. No FIA or F1 primary document records a particular pass as an Overtake Mode pass, so the script should present it as a quote, not as a documented lap.

Backup: the 2026 Australian GP. Russell and Leclerc swapped the lead 6 times in 9 laps (ESPN; Sky Sports says 7). The Race attributes the passes to Boost "or" Overtake Mode, so the example mixes in Boost, which the brief excludes. Don't use it for this episode.

## Unverified / do not use
- **The current Section C text of the taper formulas.** F9 and F10 rest on FIA PU regs Issue 6 (Mar 2024) plus media. I could see only the contents page of the latest Section C (Issue 15, 10 Dec 2025; MGU-K is C5.18), not its formula. No source reports a change. Before lock, QA should check C5.18 in Issue 15 or later.
- **Where normal deployment reaches zero**: 345 km/h (FIA Issue 6 formula), 355 km/h (ESPN and others) or 340 km/h (another explainer). Sources conflict, so do not put a zero speed on the normal line.
- **"Overtake Mode usable anywhere on the lap."** Sky Sports and RacingNews365 say it can be spread over the lap. The FIA Dutch and Monza documents seem to list specific "overtake sectors" (Zandvoort about 350–800 m, 2000–2450 m, 3100–3400 m). It is unclear whether those limit where the extra power can be used. Say "on the next lap" only.
- **Who triggers Overtake Mode.** F1 Chronicle says the ECU manages it automatically. Other sources say the driver chooses when to deploy it. Do not say "button" or "automatic".
- **A universal 1 s gap in the regulations.** Sportrules.org says the rule text sets no universal figure: the FIA states the detection gap per event. It was 1 s at every event I checked (Miami, Monaco, Monza, Melbourne), so "within 1 s" is safe as the 2026 practice. Do not write "the rules say 1 s".
- **The overtake-count stat.** "Near-tripling in overtakes" at Australia 2026 (F1 social media, via The Race; Sky gives 120 overtakes) is not an FIA figure, and The Race questioned it. The brief's Avoid list rules it out. Do not use.
- **DRS debut race.** One source says the 2011 Spanish GP, which is very likely wrong because 2011 Australia was the opener. Only use "2011".
- **Any change to Overtake Mode after Monza (Sep 2026).** I found no reported change to the +0.5 MJ or 337 km/h figures after the Miami package, but searches for July–October amendments came back thin. The reported Sporting Regs issue "08, 5 Aug 2026" (sportrules.org) could not be checked.
- **Speed differences between cars in km/h** (e.g. "30+ km/h closing speeds"). These belong to Boost and superclipping, not Overtake Mode. Do not use.
